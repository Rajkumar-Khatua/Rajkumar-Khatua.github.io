Your Zoho Books Data Is Telling a Story. Can You See It?

A business can have all its financial data in one place and still struggle to answer a simple question:

How healthy is the business right now?

The invoices are there.

The payments are there.

The customers are there.

The expenses are there.

But the answers leadership needs often aren't.

That was the problem I wanted to solve.

The problem wasn't the data. It was the visibility.

Zoho Books already captures a huge amount of financial information.

But looking at individual accounting reports doesn't always reveal the bigger picture.

Leadership may want to know:

  • How much cash do we really have?
  • How much money is stuck in receivables?
  • Which customers are paying slowly?
  • How much revenue is waiting to be invoiced?
  • Where is revenue coming from?
  • Where is the business spending money?

Instead of answering these questions one report at a time, I wanted to bring them together into a single financial intelligence layer.

From Zoho Books to Financial Visibility

I built an executive finance analytics dashboard using Zoho Books and Zoho Analytics.

The idea was simple:

Take the financial data the business already has → connect the important signals → turn them into information leadership can act on.

The dashboard connects:

Revenue → Customers → Receivables → Cash → Pipeline → Payments → Costs

The Executive Snapshot brings five critical signals together:

  • Cash on Hand
  • Usable Cash
  • Gross Margin
  • Draft Pipeline
  • AR Overdue %

The demonstration dashboard shows ₹267.73L cash on hand, ₹211.35L usable cash after tax, 79.59% gross margin, ₹179.42L draft pipeline, and 15.25% AR overdue.

Then the numbers started telling a story.

₹179.42L was sitting in draft invoices.

That's not simply a pipeline number.

It's potential revenue that hadn't yet entered the formal invoicing and collection cycle.

At the margin assumption used in the dashboard, that represented approximately ₹1.15Cr of potential profit sitting unbilled.

The question changed from:

How much revenue do we have?

to:

How much value have we created that hasn't been converted into billed revenue yet?

Then came the receivables problem.

The dashboard showed ₹56.1L in overdue receivables, with ₹18.3L sitting beyond 60 days.

That's where the revenue story becomes a cash-flow story.

A business can invoice strongly and still have cash trapped with customers.

So the dashboard doesn't stop at total receivables.

It shows where the risk is, how old the outstanding balance is, and how customers behave when it comes to payment.

Customers aren't just revenue numbers.

The deeper Customer 360 view combines:

Total Invoiced + Total Overdue + Total Draft + Average Days to Pay + Payment Count

in one customer-level view.

That changes how a business can look at its customers.

A customer can be:

  • A major revenue contributor
  • A collection risk
  • A future billing opportunity
  • A fast or slow payer

And sometimes, they can be all four.

The sample data shows customers with average payment times ranging from roughly 7–10 days to 16–19.5 days, making payment behavior visible alongside revenue and outstanding balances.

And cash tells another part of the story.

Revenue doesn't automatically mean liquidity.

The dashboard therefore looks at cash by account and cash concentration.

In the demonstration data, 63.2% of the liquid cash shown was in Undeposited Funds, compared with 20.8% in the current account and 15.9% in the savings reserve.

This gives leadership another question to investigate:

We have cash — but where exactly is it sitting?

One view. Different questions.

The final analytics layer connects the financial story across:

Revenue

Understand revenue trends and service-line contribution.

Cash

See cash position and where liquid funds are concentrated.

Receivables

Identify overdue balances and aging-related collection risk.

Customers

Understand revenue, overdue exposure, draft pipeline and payment behavior together.

Pipeline

See potential billing sitting in draft invoices.

Profitability

Understand COGS and operating expense categories.

The goal wasn't to create a dashboard full of charts.

The goal was to make the financial story visible.

The approach

I started with the business questions rather than the visualizations.

1. Understand the decisions

What does leadership actually need to know?

2. Define the KPIs

Which metrics can answer those questions?

3. Connect the data

Bring the relevant Zoho Books information together.

4. Build the analytical layer

Create the calculations, relationships and customer-level views needed for analysis.

5. Design the dashboard

Present the information around decisions — not around whatever charts happen to look good.
